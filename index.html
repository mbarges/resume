Meredith Barges


Meredith Barges, MDiv, PhD (ABD)

Bird-friendly Building Policy Researcher & Advocate

LinkedIn/ CV / Bio

I am a bird-friendly building policy researcher investigating why U.S. and Canadian cities are increasingly planning and building with wild birds in mind. My research explores how birds have come to be recognized as municipal political subjects worthy, in their own rights, of municipal policy benefits and privileges in how the city is designed. Municipal bird-friendly building standards reflect a changing view of the city, from ecological sacrifice zones to biodiverse spaces where wildlife and humans can thrive together. This has important implications for land use, design/architecture, urban planning, and sustainable development — as well as human-nature relations.

From 2022-2023, I served as the policy researcher for the Yale Bird-friendly Building Initiative, a collaborative project of Yale Law School and American Bird Conservancy. In that role, I co-authored Building Safer Cities for Birds: How Cities Are Leading the Way on Bird-Friendly Building Policy, the first reoport to analyze bird-friendly building lawmaking.

As a PhD candidate at SUNY ESF, my research focuses on municipal bird-friendly building policymaking in the U.S. and Canada. Combining public policy analysis and environmental ethics, I am seeking to explain why some U.S. and Canadian cities have bird-friendly building policies and not others. In particular, I'm interested in what happens when birds move from being objects of national/state conservation regimes to municipal policy beneficiaries with specific claims on the built environment.

Since 2024, in collaboration with the SUNY ESF Office of Sustainability and the ESF Birding Club, I have helped to make SUNY ESF's Syracuse campus more bird friendly. This started with the passage of ESF's new mandatory bird-friendly building policy, incorporated into ESF's Sustainability Action Plan. I also led a community-based retrofit pilot project that hass seen several large glass windscreens on our Syracuse campus retrofitted with bird-themed "mitigation murals," to reduce bird building collisions. I also supported the new iNaturalist SUNY ESF Community Bird Collision Project, used to track collisions on campus. Prior to ESF, I was actively promoting bird-friendly practices at Yale University. There I led a successful campaign to convince Yale Divinity School to join the National Audubon Society's Lights Out program for migrating birds. Both SUNY ESF and Yale University have recognized my bird conservation advocacy with awards in public service and sustainability.

As an advocate, I promote municipal and state policies to make the built environment safer for birds. This includes pushing for passage of bird-friendly building standards, Dark Sky lighting practices, native landscaping, and alternatives to toxic rodenticides. I co-founded and co-chaired Lights Out Connecticut and helped to lead a successful statewide campaign to pass Connecticut’s Lights Out Law (Public Act 23-143), which requires state agencies to turn off all unneccessary lights by 11pm—for birds! From 2024-26, I chaired Lights Out Central New York, a nonprofit project of the Onondaga Audubon Society dedicated to making the night sky darker and safer for migrating birds.

I hold an MDiv from Yale Divinity School and an M.A. in history from the University of Chicago.
