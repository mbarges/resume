Meredith Barges


Meredith Barges, MDiv, PhD (ABD)

Bird-friendly Building Policy Researcher & Advocate

CV / LinkedIn

I am a advocate committed to advancing bird-friendly building solutions for urban bird populations. I've been recognized for leading institutional change, with a proven track record of policy adoption, community moblization, and mitigation projects to protect birds from fatal building collisions.

My work sits at the intersection of urban sustainability, bird conservation, ethics, and green building standards. As an applied conservation researcher, I have led and supported policy efforts at the state and local levels. In collaboration with multiple local partners, I co-led the successful statewide campaign that secured passage of Connecticut’s Public Act 23-143, a landmark “lights out” law requiring state agencies to turn off unnecessary nighttime lighting at public buildings for birds. As an expert on bird-friendly building policymaking, I regularly consult and partner with local advocates on lighting and zoning code improvements, to establish exemplary lighting regulations that support birds--and people. I co-founded Lights Out Connecticut in 2022; and chaired Lights Out Central New York from 2023-2026.

At SUNY ESF and Yale University, I translated my knowledge and passion for bird conservation into tangible bird-friendly building protections. I helped to secure a mandatory bird-friendly building policy at the SUNY ESF as part of its 2025 Sustainability Action Plan, and led a community-driven, art-based retrofit initiative that reduced bird collisions on campus. I also led the successful campaign in 2021 to convince Yale Divinity School to join Audobon's Lights Out program. These efforts reflect my commitment to community and home based bird conservation.

Underpinning this work is my doctoral research. My PhD research project examines the dynamics driving the adoption—and non-adoption—of municipal bird-friendly building regulations in the United States and Canada. Combining policy analysis, public policy theory, environmental ethics, and urban political ecology, I analyze the factors explaining why some cities adopt meaningful protections for urban birds, while others don't. In parallel, I work closely with policymakers, advocates, and community partners to translate ecological science into actionable strategies, bridging the gap between science and real-world impacts.
