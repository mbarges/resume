Meredith Barges


Meredith Barges, MDiv, PhD (ABD)

Bird-friendly Building Policy Researcher & Advocate

LinkedIn/ CV / Bio

I am a bird-friendly building policy researcher and advocate. My research explores how municipal governments are increasingly recognizing birds as worthy policy subjects deserving specialized benefits, from where development occurs to how buildings are designed. Municipal bird-friendly policymaking interests me because it reflects a changing view of the rightful place (or belonging) of wild birds in the city as well as the reimagination of the city as part of the wider wilds, with implications for bird conservation, sustainable development, biodiverity governance, and design/architecture.

From 2022-2023, I served as the policy researcher for the Yale Bird-friendly Building Initiative, a collaborative project of Yale Law School and American Bird Conservancy. In that role, I co-authored Building Safer Cities for Birds: How Cities Are Leading the Way on Bird-Friendly Building Policy, a first-of-its-kind report analyzing bird-friendly building policymaking in five U.S. cities.

My PhD project at SUNY ESF focuses on municipal bird-friendly building policymaking in the U.S. and Canada. I seek to explain why some U.S. and Canadian cities have bird-friendly building policies, while others don't. In particular, I'm interested in what happens when birds move from being objects of national/state conservation regimes to municipal subjects with recognized claims on city planning and building decisions.

I hold an M.A. in history from the University of Chicagon and an MDiv in religion and ecology from Yale Divinity School.
