Meredith Barges

Meredith Barges, MDiv, PhD (ABD)

Bird-friendly Building Policy Researcher & Advocate

CV / LinkedIn


I am dynamic, results-driven advocate for urban birds advancing innovative policy solutions at the intersection of urban sustainability, biodiversity conservation, and the built environment. I have been recognized for leading high-impact legislation and institutional change, with a proven track record of policy adoption, community moblization, and tangible results for birds.

As a conservation researcher, I work at the intersection of applied science and advocacy. I help to turn rigorous research findings into policy change that reshapes how cities are built and lit. My work has delivered tangible conservation outcomes, from statewide legislation to institutional reform, while advancing a broader vision of urban environments that are safer for birds and healthier for people.

I have led and supported policy efforts that produce measurable results. I co-led the statewide campaign that secured passage of Connecticut’s Public Act 23-143, a landmark “lights out” law requiring state agencies to turn off unnecessary nighttime lighting at public buildings for birds. I also partner with local advocates to advance comprehensive lighting and zoning code changes, to establishing exemplary municipal lighting regulations.

At SUNY ESF and Yale University, I have translated my advocacy into tangible bird-friendly building protections. I helped secure a mandatory bird-friendly building policy at the SUNY ESF as part of its 2025 Sustainability Action Plan, and led a community-driven, art-based retrofit initiative that reduced bird collisions on campus. These efforts reflect my commitment to community and home based bird conservation.

Underpinning this work is my doctoral research. My PhD research project examines the dynamics driving the adoption—and non-adoption—of mandatory bird-friendly building regulations in the United States and Canada. Drawing on policy analysis, policy theory, environmental ethics, and urban political ecology, I analyze the factors explaining why some cities adopt meaningful protections for urban birds, while others don't. In parallel, I work closely with policymakers, advocates, and community partners to translate ecological science into actionable strategies, bridging the gap between science and real-world impacts.
